# Market: United States

Pages: `/markets/united-states`, `/markets/united-states/{texas,california,florida}`

## Regulatory context (what actually applies)

- **Accounting standard:** GAAP. SOX applies to large/public filers, not the typical Accounstone client.
- **Tax:** Federal + state tax return preparation. IRS is the federal tax authority.
- **Representation:** IRS representation (Form 2848 power of attorney) requires a licensed CPA, Enrolled Agent, or attorney under Circular 230. Accounstone does not hold this credential as an entity and must never claim to provide IRS representation. Returns are prepared for the client's CPA/EA to review, sign, and file.
- **Audit:** GAAS / PCAOB standards for audit work (preparation only — see `knowledge/services/audit-support.md`).

## Terminology notes

- "Franchise tax" (e.g., Texas) is a state-level tax distinct from federal income tax — don't conflate with income tax terminology used in other markets.
- "Sales tax nexus" is the US-specific term for multi-state tax registration triggers (equivalent concept to UK VAT registration thresholds or AU GST registration, but mechanically different — nexus is transaction/revenue-based per state, not a single national threshold).

## Buyer expectations

US buyers (CPA firms and businesses) expect: QuickBooks Online or Xero as the bookkeeping platform, CCH Axcess or Drake Tax on the tax-practice side, and a bookkeeping/close cycle that feeds a CPA's tax preparation rather than replacing it. CPA firms are the primary ICP in this market — see `knowledge/icp/cpa-firms.md`.
